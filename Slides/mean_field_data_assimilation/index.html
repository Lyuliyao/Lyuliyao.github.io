Motivation

Mean-field reduction: discard labels, then let \(N\to\infty\)

1 · Labeled system

\(N\) interacting agents

\[ \intd \bX^i_t = \frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N K(\bX^j_t-\bX^i_t)\,\intd t + \Sigma\,\intd\bW^i_t . \]
Discard the agent labels
2 · Measure-valued state

Keep only the empirical measure

\[ \mu_t^N=\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N\delta_{\bX_t^j} \]
The interaction depends on the population only through \(\mu_t^N\). Relabeling or reordering the agents leaves the dynamics unchanged.
Let N → ∞
3 · Mean-field limit

One particle coupled to its law

\[ \intd \bX_t = \bb(\bX_t,\mu_t)\,\intd t + \Sigma\,\intd\bW_t, \qquad \mu_t=\Law(\bX_t). \]
Build the reduction in three steps. Start with the labeled particle system. Next discard arbitrary labels by representing the population with its empirical measure; the interaction depends on the population only through that measure. Finally take the large-population limit to obtain the McKean–Vlasov law. Keep the setup conceptual here: the observed object is a collection of particle trajectories, while the unknown is the complete map from a state and a population law to the drift. Introduce the empirical-measure and finite-difference notation later, when the training pairs and loss are discussed. Following the paper's introduction, emphasize three obstacles. First, explicit all-pairs evaluation costs O(N squared) for N particles. Second, an additive pairwise ansatz is linear in the empirical measure and cannot represent nonlinear many-body effects beyond a superposition of two-body forces; normalized interactions are one example, not the main summary. Third, a standard MLP is a finite-dimensional vector-to-vector map, with a dimension fixed by N, and is order-sensitive unless symmetry is imposed. Neural operators instead learn maps between function spaces, typically for functions defined on structured domains. The mean-field drift has a different domain: its second input is a probability measure. These requirements motivate an architecture defined directly on measures. Part I — learning the drift

Measure-Valued Neural Network (MVNN)

Two neural networks connected by one population average:  \(\bb_\theta(\bx,\mu)=\varphi_\intn\big(\bx,\ \langle\varphi_\emb,\mu\rangle\big)\).
Key idea. The population enters only through the averaged feature \(\bz_\mu\) — permutation invariant by construction, one embedding pass per particle: \(O(N)\), not \(O(N^2)\).
MVNN stands for Measure-Valued Neural Network. Read the diagram from left to right. The same embedding network is applied independently to every particle. Averaging the resulting features produces a finite population representation z_mu. The interaction network then combines the query state x with z_mu to produce the drift. Because the population enters through one average, the architecture is permutation invariant and its particle evaluation is linear in N. Training minimizes the velocity MSE; the detailed empirical-measure and finite-difference notation can be introduced here verbally or on a later training slide. Part I — learning the drift · guarantees 1/3

The learned drift defines a genuine mean-field model

PropositionWell-posedness of the learned dynamics
If \(\varphi_\emb\) and \(\varphi_\intn\) are globally Lipschitz and \(f_0\in\mathcal P_2(\Rd)\), then for any \(T>0\) the learned McKean–Vlasov SDE \[ \intd\bX^\theta_t=\bb_\theta\big(\bX^\theta_t,f^\theta_t\big)\,\intd t+\sigma\,\intd\bB_t, \qquad f^\theta_t=\Law(\bX^\theta_t), \] has a unique strong solution on \([0,T]\), and \(f^\theta_t\) is the unique weak solution of the associated nonlinear Fokker–Planck equation. Meaning: trained weights give a well-defined continuum model — no smoothing or filtering of the data is needed.
PropositionPropagation of chaos for the learned particle system
Under the same assumptions, let \((\bX^{\theta,i,N}_t)_{i=1}^N\) solve the learned \(N\)-particle system with \(f_0\)-chaotic initial data. Then for any \(T>0\) the particle law is \(f^\theta\)-chaotic: \[ \lim_{N\to\infty}W_2\big(f^{1,\theta,N}_{[0,T]},\,f^{\theta}_{[0,T]}\big)=0 . \] Meaning: the \(N\)-particle simulation and the mean-field model agree as \(N\) grows — train at one \(N\), simulate at any \(N\).
Standard McKean–Vlasov theory applies because the MVNN drift is Lipschitz in the state and in the measure (in W2). Propagation of chaos is by synchronous coupling and Grönwall.
Part I — learning the drift · guarantees 2/3

How expressive is the architecture?

TheoremUniversal approximation (after Pham–Warin)
Let \(\zeta\) be a probability measure on \(\Ptwo\) and \(\bb^\star:\Rd\times\Ptwo\to\Rd\) continuous with \(\|\bb^\star\|_{L^2(\zeta)}<\infty\). For every \(\varepsilon>0\) there exist \(k\in\mathbb N\) and networks \(\varphi_\emb:\Rd\to\mathbb R^k\), \(\varphi_\intn:\Rd\times\mathbb R^k\to\Rd\) such that \[ \big\|\bb^\star-\varphi_\intn\big(\cdot,\langle\varphi_\emb,\cdot\rangle\big)\big\|_{L^2(\zeta)}\le\varepsilon . \]
TheoremQuantitative approximation via finite-dimensional measure embeddings
Let \(f:\Omega\times U\to\mathbb R\) be Lipschitz in the state and in \(W_1\), with all measures supported in \(\Omega=[-\gamma_1,\gamma_1]^d\). Cover \(\Omega\) by \(C_\Omega=\varepsilon^{-d}\) balls, take a partition of unity \(\{\omega_m\}\) subordinate to it, and embed \(\mu\mapsto\bu=\big(\langle\omega_1,\mu\rangle,\dots,\langle\omega_{C_\Omega},\mu\rangle\big)\). Then there are coefficients \(a_k\) and ReLU networks \(q_k\) with explicit depth, sparsity and weight bounds such that \[ \sup_{\bx\in\Omega,\,\mu\in U}\Big|f(\bx,\mu)-\sum_{k=1}^{H}a_k\,q_k(\bx,\bu)\Big|\le\varepsilon, \qquad H=O\Big(\big((C_\Omega+d)\,C_\Omega d\big)^{\frac{C_\Omega+d}{2}}\,\varepsilon^{-C_\Omega-d}\Big). \] Meaning: exactly MVNN’s form — a finite embedding of \(\mu\) feeding an interaction network — made fully constructive. But a fixed histogram-type embedding needs \(C_\Omega=\varepsilon^{-d}\) features, and \(H\) blows up accordingly: the curse of dimensionality when \(\bb\) may be an arbitrary functional of \(\mu\).
UAP comes from the density of cylindrical functionals on Wasserstein space. The quantitative theorem makes the same form constructive with a fixed partition-of-unity (histogram-type) embedding: everything is explicit — depth and sparsity polynomial in d+C_Omega, but C_Omega = epsilon^{-d} features are needed and the number of networks H and the weight bounds blow up like epsilon^{-C_Omega}. That worst case motivates the structural assumption on the next slide.
Part I — learning the drift · guarantees 3/3

Approximation rate under low-dimensional measure dependence

AssumptionFinite-dimensional measure dependency
On the family \(U\) of measures of interest, every functional is determined by \(r\) fixed feature functions: \[ V(\mu)=G\big(\langle g_1,\mu\rangle,\dots,\langle g_r,\mu\rangle\big)\qquad\forall\,\mu\in U . \] Meaning: \(U\) lies on a finite-dimensional manifold inside \(\Ptwo\). Gaussians are the model case — determined by mean and covariance, \(r=d+d(d+1)/2\); flocking, consensus and aggregation are governed by a few order parameters.
TheoremApproximation rate of MVNN under the assumption
Suppose the drift depends on the measure only through the \(r\) features, \(\bb^\star(\bx,\mu)=G\big(\bx,\langle g_1,\mu\rangle,\dots,\langle g_r,\mu\rangle\big)\) on \([0,1]^d\) with \(G\) and the \(g_i\) Lipschitz. Then for every \(\varepsilon>0\) there are deep ReLU networks achieving \(\sup_{\bx,\mu}\|\bb^\star-\bb_\theta\|\le\varepsilon\) with \[ \text{depth}=O(\log\varepsilon^{-1}),\qquad W_\intn=O\big(\varepsilon^{-(d+r)}\big),\qquad W_\emb=O\big(\varepsilon^{-d}\big). \] Meaning: the embedding network learns the \(r\) order parameters instead of the \(\varepsilon^{-d}\) fixed features of the previous theorem — the complexity is governed by \(d+r\), and the curse disappears.
This is the reason MVNN is practical: flocking, consensus and aggregation are governed by a few macroscopic order parameters, so r is small. The Gaussian family is the canonical example of the assumption. Contrast with the previous slide: there the embedding was fixed and of size epsilon^{-d}; here the network learns the r features, and the rate is polynomial in epsilon^{-1} with exponent d+r.

From learning to assimilation

A learned model still drifts

The example: a real fish school

\(N\approx1{,}126\) fish in a shallow tank, filmed from above and tracked frame by frame (TRex). The school circulates along the tank wall.

What is actually observed

Tracks are incomplete (occlusions, dropouts) and identities are not consistent across frames — so the observation is the density: macroscopic, unlabeled, permutation invariant.

Open loop drifts

An MVNN drift trained on this dataset (Part I) captures the mean circulation, but bias + initialization error make the free rollout lose the school within seconds.
Needed. Compare distributions, then nudge particles.
This is the hinge of the talk: the object learned in Part I becomes the biased model of Part II. In the fish example, the learned drift captures circulation but loses coherence within seconds. The tracker is TRex; at every frame a substantial fraction of trajectories carry a missing flag. Conventional methods face a cross-scale mismatch: forecasts are labeled configurations while observations are functionals of the law. EnKF uses labeled-coordinate covariances; 4D-Var requires a non-unique lift from density to configuration; particle filters suffer weight degeneracy; and particle-flow filters typically require likelihoods on labeled states.

Part II — Multiscale Nudging

True vs. model dynamics, and what is observed

True process \(\bX_t\) and model \(\bY_t\): shared noise channel, bias in the drift

\[ \intd\bX_t=\btrue(\bX_t,\mu_t)\intd t+\Sigma\intd\bW_t,\qquad \intd\bY_t=\bmodel(\bY_t,\nu_t)\intd t+\Sigma\intd\bW_t,\qquad \mathbf R:=\bmodel-\btrue . \]

Smoothing kernel \(K_h(\bx)=h^{-d}K(\bx/h)\) (Gaussian), even; the bandwidth \(h\) is the resolution of the measurement device.

Case A — pixel / density observation

\[ \muobs_t(\bz)=(K_h*\mu_t)(\bz). \] Imaging, satellite products, reconstructed densities.

Case B — unlabeled particle locations

\[ \muobs_t(\bz)=\tfrac1M\sum_{k=1}^M K_h\big(\bz-\bX^{\obs,k,M}_t\big). \] Tracking with dropouts, no index matching across frames.
Both cases enter the same framework through the same object, the measure \(\muobs\) — and the forecast is compared with it only after the same smoothing \(K_h\).
Keep the diffusion shared so the bias is in the drift. The two observation modalities plug into one formula.
Part II — Multiscale Nudging

Nudging as the Wasserstein gradient of a measure-level misfit

Misfit at the observation scale, and its first variation (\(K_h\) even \(\Rightarrow\) self-adjoint)

\[ J_{\mathrm{nud},h}(\nu,\muobs)=\tfrac12\!\int_{\Rd}\big|K_h*\nu-\muobs\big|^2\intd\bx,\qquad \phi_{\nu,h}:=\frac{\delta J_{\mathrm{nud},h}}{\delta\nu}=\widetilde K_h*\nu-K_h*\muobs,\qquad \widetilde K_h:=K_h*K_h . \]

Steepest descent in Wasserstein geometry (JKO minimizing movement, \(\muobs\) frozen) gives a velocity field on state space

\[ \nu^{+}\in\arg\min_{\rho\in\mathcal P_2}\Big\{\tfrac{1}{2\tau}W_2^2(\rho,\nu)+\lambda J_{\mathrm{nud},h}(\rho,\muobs)\Big\} \quad\xrightarrow{\ \tau\to0\ }\quad \bu_{\mathrm{nud}}(\bz)=-\lambda\nabla\phi_{\nu,h}(\bz)=-\lambda\nabla\big(\widetilde K_h*\nu-K_h*\muobs\big)(\bz). \]

Assimilated dynamics — its law solves the nonlinear Fokker–Planck equation with the extra flux \(\lambda\nu_t\nabla(K_h*r_t)\), \(r_t=K_h*\nu_t-\muobs_t\)

\[ \intd\bZ_t=\bmodel(\bZ_t,\nu_t)\intd t-\lambda\nabla\big(\widetilde K_h*\nu_t-K_h*\muobs_t\big)(\bZ_t)\intd t+\Sigma\intd\bW_t . \]
Structure preserving. If \(\bmodel=-\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\nu}\) and \(\Sigma=\sqrt{2\beta}\,I\), the assimilated flow is itself the Wasserstein gradient flow of \(\mathcal F_t(\nu)=F(\nu)+\lambda J_{\mathrm{nud},h}(\nu,\muobs_t)+\beta\,\mathrm{Ent}(\nu)\): nudging adds an observation-driven, time-dependent potential.
Smoothing by K_h restores enough regularity for the velocity to be defined when nu is only a measure, and matches the observation scale.

Part II — Multiscale Nudging

Guarantees: well posed, mean-field consistent, error contracting

PropositionsWell-posedness; propagation of chaos
\(\bmodel\) Lipschitz in \((\bx,W_2)\) and \(\nabla\widetilde K_h\) Lipschitz \(\Rightarrow\) unique strong solution for any \(T\) and \(\Law(\bZ_0)\in\mathcal P_2\). \(\|\nabla\widetilde K_h\|_\infty<\infty\) \(\Rightarrow\) the nudged \(N\)-particle system is \(f\)-chaotic: \(W_2\big(f^{1,N}_{[0,T]},f_{[0,T]}\big)\to0\). (Synchronous coupling + Grönwall; for the Gaussian kernel \(\widetilde K_h\in C^\infty\) and \(\|\nabla v-\nabla(\widetilde K_h*v)\|_{L^2}\le Ch\|D^2v\|_{L^2}\).)
Theorem\(L^2\) decay to a bias floor — \(\Omega=\Tor\), \(A=\tfrac12\Sigma\Sigma^\top\succeq\kappa I\), exact smoothed observations \(\muobs_t=K_h*\mu_t\), \(\int(\nu_0-\mu_0)=0\)
Let \(e_t=\nu_t-\mu_t\), \(V(t)=\tfrac12\|e_t\|^2_{L^2}\). Assume (C1) \(\|\bmodel(\cdot,\mu)-\bmodel(\cdot,\nu)\|_{L^2}\le L_\mu\|\mu-\nu\|_{L^2}\); (C2) \(\mu_t,\nu_t\le\bar\rho\), \(\nu_t\ge\underline\nu>0\); (C3) \(\|\nabla e_t-\nabla(\widetilde K_h*e_t)\|_{L^2}\le\delta(h)\|\nabla e_t\|_{L^2}\), \(\delta(h)\to0\). With \(C_{\rm adv}=B_{\rm model}+\bar\rho L_\mu\), model bias \(\Delta=\sup_t\|\bmodel(\cdot,\mu_t)-\btrue(\cdot,\mu_t)\|_{L^\infty}\) and Poincaré constant \(C_P\): if \[ \delta(h)\le\frac{\underline\nu}{2\bar\rho} \quad\text{and}\quad \lambda>\frac{1}{\underline\nu}\Big(\frac{2C_{\rm adv}^2C_P}{\kappa}-\kappa\Big), \qquad\text{then}\qquad V(t)\le e^{-\alpha t}V(0)+\frac{\bar\rho^{\,2}}{\alpha\kappa}\Delta^2, \qquad \alpha=\frac{\kappa+\lambda\underline\nu}{C_P}-\frac{2C_{\rm adv}^2}{\kappa}>0 . \]
Reading. Nudging contributes a dissipation \(-\lambda\underline\nu\|\nabla e\|^2\) that beats the advective growth \(C_{\rm adv}\|\nabla e\|\|e\|\) (Poincaré); (C3) says the kernel does not hide the error at scale \(h\); the long-time error is a bias floor \(\propto\Delta^2\) — zero for an exact model. Noisy or finite-sample observations add residual terms.
Larger λ beats advective instability; smaller h shrinks δ(h). Positivity of ν_t is guaranteed on finite intervals by a separate proposition.

Part III — moment nudging for Vlasov–Poisson

Plasmas: only hydrodynamic moments are observed

Vlasov–Poisson with collisions, \(\partial_t f+\bv\cdot\nabla_\bx f+\bE\cdot\nabla_\bv f=Q[f]\), \(-\Delta\phi=\rho-1\) on \(\Tor\times\mathbb R^d\). PIC: \(f^{N_p}=\sum_i w_i\,\delta_{(\bX^i,\bV^i)}\) — the particles are quadrature points of one law, not candidate states.

Observed: smoothed conserved moments (equivalently \(\rho,\bu,T\)), linear in \(f\)

\[ \big(\rho_h,\bj_h,\mathcal K_h\big)[f](\bx)=\int K_h(\bx-\by)\,\big(1,\bv,\tfrac12|\bv|^2\big)\,f(\by,\bv)\,\intd\by\,\intd\bv \] residuals \(r_0=\rho_h[f]-\rho^\obs,\ \mathbf r_1=\bj_h[f]-\bj^\obs,\ r_2=\mathcal K_h[f]-\mathcal K^\obs\), with \(\bj^\obs=\rho^\obs\bu^\obs\), \(\mathcal K^\obs=\tfrac12\rho^\obs|\bu^\obs|^2+\tfrac d2\rho^\obs T^\obs\).
Moment-compatible set \(\mathcal S_h=\{f: r_0=\mathbf r_1=r_2=0\}\ni f^\dagger\): the data fix a family, not a member. Needed: a lift from the moment residuals to a particle drift that sees the particles only through \(\mu^{N_p}\) and vanishes on all of \(\mathcal S_h\).
A two-stream state can have exactly the moments of a Maxwellian. The observations determine only the family. Nudging the moment equations directly would need a closure — the hierarchy is not closed at the observed level.
Part III — moment nudging for Vlasov–Poisson

What moment feedback cannot do: the two-Maxwellian obstruction

PropositionTwo-Maxwellian obstruction
Consider any residual-driven assimilation \(\partial_t f+\bv\cdot\nabla_\bx f+\bE[f]\cdot\nabla_\bv f=Q[f]+\mathcal N\big(f,\mathcal O[f]-\mathcal O[f^\dagger]\big)\) on the neutral torus. Assume (A1) \(Q[\mathcal M_{1,\bu,T}]=0\) for every homogeneous Maxwellian; (A2) \(\mathcal N(f,0)=0\); (A3) there are \((\bu_1,T_1)\neq(\bu_2,T_2)\) with \(\mathcal O[\mathcal M_{1,\bu_1,T_1}]=\mathcal O[\mathcal M_{1,\bu_2,T_2}]\); (A4) uniqueness. If \(f^\dagger(0)=\mathcal M_{1,\bu_1,T_1}\) and \(f(0)=\mathcal M_{1,\bu_2,T_2}\), then both stay put for all time and \(d\big(f(t),f^\dagger(t)\big)\) is constant for every metric \(d\).
CorollaryNecessity of moment observability
Synchronization from arbitrary Maxwellian initializations requires \((\bu,T)\mapsto\mathcal O[\mathcal M_{1,\bu,T}]\) to be injective: an observation map that does not distinguish bulk velocity and temperature (e.g. density alone) cannot synchronize.
Why. Linearized around a Maxwellian, moment-conserving collisions are coercive on the complement of \(\mathrm{span}\{1,\bv,|\bv|^2\}\): they damp the non-hydrodynamic shape error but leave the hydrodynamic variables untouched. What collisions do not damp, the data must observe — so the feedback is built from all three residuals \((r_0,\mathbf r_1,r_2)\).
This is a design constraint, proved before any method: any feedback that only sees residuals inherits this obstruction.
Part III — moment nudging for Vlasov–Poisson

Method A: quadratic mismatch + velocity-weighted Wasserstein metric

\[ J_M[f]=\sum_{k=0}^{2}\tfrac{\gamma_{k+1}}{2}\|r_k\|_{L^2}^2,\qquad \Phi_M[f]=\frac{\delta J_M}{\delta f}=\gamma_1K_h*r_0+\gamma_2\,\bv\cdot K_h*\mathbf r_1+\tfrac{\gamma_3}{2}|\bv|^2K_h*r_2 . \]
Plain \(W_2\) descent \(-\nabla_{\bx,\bv}\Phi_M\): the position drift contains \(\tfrac{\gamma_3}{2}|\bv|^2\nabla_\bx(K_h*r_2)\) — quadratic in \(\bv\), not globally Lipschitz, outside standard well-posedness theory.

Fix the geometry, not the functional: weight spatial transport by the peculiar speed relative to the observed flow (Benamou–Brenier)

\[ G_t(\bx,\bv)=\mathrm{diag}\Big(\big(1+\tfrac{|\bv-\bu^\obs|^2}{V_*^2}\big)I_d,\;I_d\Big), \qquad \bb_A:=-G_t^{-1}\nabla_{\bx,\bv}\Phi_M=\Big(-\frac{\nabla_\bx\Phi_M}{1+|\bv-\bu^\obs|^2/V_*^2},\;-\nabla_\bv\Phi_M\Big), \] then \(\dot{\bX}^i=\bV^i+\bb_{A,\bx}\), \(\dot{\bV}^i=\bE(\bX^i)+\bb_{A,\bv}\) (+ collision map). The weight absorbs the \(|\bv|^2\) growth; the velocity drift is affine in \(\bv\).
Two structural facts. \(\nabla_{\bx,\bv}\Phi_M[f]=0\) \(f\)-a.e. \(\iff f\in\mathcal S_h\) (the feedback vanishes exactly on the compatible set); with frozen observations \(\frac{\intd}{\intd t}J_M[f_t]=-\|\mathrm{grad}_{G}J_M\|^2_{g_{f_t}}\le0\) (Lyapunov).
The peculiar speed is Galilean invariant and equals the intrinsic one on the compatible set. V_* is a thermal speed scale.

Part III — moment nudging · the limit case

Limits of moment feedback: the two-stream instability

Design. Weak BGK collisions (\(\nu=0.05\)), no driver — the instability saturates into a phase-space vortex, so the shape is neither observed nor damped.
Truth. \(f^\dagger_0\propto v^2e^{-v^2/2}(1+\alpha\cos k_0x)\), moments \((\rho,u,T)=(1,0,3)\).
