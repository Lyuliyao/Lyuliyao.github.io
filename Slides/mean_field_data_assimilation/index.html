Motivation

Mean-field reduction: discard labels, then let \(N\to\infty\)

1 · Labeled system

\(N\) interacting agents

\[ \intd \bX^i_t = \frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N K(\bX^j_t-\bX^i_t)\,\intd t + \Sigma\,\intd\bW^i_t . \]
Discard the agent labels
2 · Measure-valued state

Keep only the empirical measure

\[ \mu_t^N=\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N\delta_{\bX_t^j} \]
The interaction depends on the population only through \(\mu_t^N\). Relabeling or reordering the agents leaves the dynamics unchanged.
Let N → ∞
3 · Mean-field limit

One particle coupled to its law

\[ \intd \bX_t = \bb(\bX_t,\mu_t)\,\intd t + \Sigma\,\intd\bW_t, \qquad \mu_t=\Law(\bX_t). \]
Build the reduction in three steps. Start with the labeled particle system. Next discard arbitrary labels by representing the population with its empirical measure; the interaction depends on the population only through that measure. Finally take the large-population limit to obtain the McKean–Vlasov law. Keep the setup conceptual here: the observed object is a collection of particle trajectories, while the unknown is the complete map from a state and a population law to the drift. Introduce the empirical-measure and finite-difference notation later, when the training pairs and loss are discussed. Following the paper's introduction, emphasize three obstacles. First, explicit all-pairs evaluation costs O(N squared) for N particles. Second, an additive pairwise ansatz is linear in the empirical measure and cannot represent nonlinear many-body effects beyond a superposition of two-body forces; normalized interactions are one example, not the main summary. Third, a standard MLP is a finite-dimensional vector-to-vector map, with a dimension fixed by N, and is order-sensitive unless symmetry is imposed. Neural operators instead learn maps between function spaces, typically for functions defined on structured domains. The mean-field drift has a different domain: its second input is a probability measure. These requirements motivate an architecture defined directly on measures. MVNN stands for Measure-Valued Neural Network. Read the diagram from left to right. The same embedding network is applied independently to every particle. Part I — learning the drift

Three guarantees for MVNN

1Well posed
Lipschitz networks define a unique McKean–Vlasov dynamics.
2Consistent across particle counts
Propagation of chaos: \(\mu_t^{\theta,N}\longrightarrow f_t^\theta\) as \(N\to\infty\).
3Expressive
For every continuous \(\bb^\star\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), MVNN can achieve \(\|\bb^\star-\bb_\theta\|_{L^2}\le\varepsilon\). With \(r\) order parameters, complexity is governed by \(d+r\).
Assume the embedding and interaction networks are globally Lipschitz. Then the learned McKean–Vlasov SDE has a unique strong solution and the learned (N)-particle system is chaotic, so a model trained at one finite (N) can be simulated at another. Universal approximation follows the cylindrical-functional construction. In general, approximation suffers from a curse of dimensionality; when the drift depends on the law through (r) order parameters, ReLU depth is (O(\log\varepsilon^{-1})), interaction width is (O(\varepsilon^{-(d+r)})), and embedding width is (O(\varepsilon^{-d})).
Part I — learning the drift

MVNN captures normalized interactions

\[ \dot X^i_t=\frac{\sum_{j}\phi(|X^j_t-X^i_t|)\,(X^j_t-X^i_t)}{\sum_{k}\phi(|X^k_t-X^i_t|)} . \]
Why it is hard. The normalization is nonlinear in \(\mu\).
Train: Gaussian-mixture laws
Test: autonomous rollouts from unseen laws
Here \(\phi(r)=e^{-(r/\ell)^2}\) with \(\ell=0.5\). Training uses \(N=16{,}000\), \(M=100\) trajectories, \(T=2\), \(\Delta t=10^{-2}\), and random Gaussian-mixture initial laws with 2–8 modes. Baselines include mean-field WSINDy, online parameter estimation, and a Gaussian-process pairwise kernel. The normalization makes the drift nonlinear in the measure, so additive and pairwise classes cannot represent it; MVNN receives no functional form.

This is the hinge of the talk: the object learned in Part I becomes the biased model of Part II. In the fish example, the learned drift captures circulation but loses coherence within seconds. Conventional methods face a cross-scale mismatch: forecasts are labeled configurations while observations are functionals of the law. Part II — Multiscale Nudging

True vs. model dynamics, and what is observed

True process \(\bX_t\) and model \(\bY_t\): shared noise channel, bias in the drift

\[ \intd\bX_t=\btrue(\bX_t,\mu_t)\intd t+\Sigma\intd\bW_t,\qquad \intd\bY_t=\bmodel(\bY_t,\nu_t)\intd t+\Sigma\intd\bW_t,\qquad \mathbf R:=\bmodel-\btrue . \]

Smoothing kernel \(K_h(\bx)=h^{-d}K(\bx/h)\) (Gaussian), even; the bandwidth \(h\) is the resolution of the measurement device.

Case A — pixel / density observation

\[ \muobs_t(\bz)=(K_h*\mu_t)(\bz). \] Imaging, satellite products, reconstructed densities.

Case B — unlabeled particle locations

\[ \muobs_t(\bz)=\tfrac1M\sum_{k=1}^M K_h\big(\bz-\bX^{\obs,k,M}_t\big). \] Tracking with dropouts, no index matching across frames.
Both cases enter the same framework through the same object, the measure \(\muobs\) — and the forecast is compared with it only after the same smoothing \(K_h\).
Keep the diffusion shared so the bias is in the drift. The two observation modalities plug into one formula.
Part II — Multiscale Nudging

Nudging as the Wasserstein gradient of a measure-level misfit

Misfit at the observation scale, and its first variation (\(K_h\) even \(\Rightarrow\) self-adjoint)

\[ J_{\mathrm{nud},h}(\nu,\muobs)=\tfrac12\!\int_{\Rd}\big|K_h*\nu-\muobs\big|^2\intd\bx,\qquad \phi_{\nu,h}:=\frac{\delta J_{\mathrm{nud},h}}{\delta\nu}=\widetilde K_h*\nu-K_h*\muobs,\qquad \widetilde K_h:=K_h*K_h . \]

Steepest descent in Wasserstein geometry (JKO minimizing movement, \(\muobs\) frozen) gives a velocity field on state space

\[ \nu^{+}\in\arg\min_{\rho\in\mathcal P_2}\Big\{\tfrac{1}{2\tau}W_2^2(\rho,\nu)+\lambda J_{\mathrm{nud},h}(\rho,\muobs)\Big\} \quad\xrightarrow{\ \tau\to0\ }\quad \bu_{\mathrm{nud}}(\bz)=-\lambda\nabla\phi_{\nu,h}(\bz)=-\lambda\nabla\big(\widetilde K_h*\nu-K_h*\muobs\big)(\bz). \]

Assimilated dynamics — its law solves the nonlinear Fokker–Planck equation with the extra flux \(\lambda\nu_t\nabla(K_h*r_t)\), \(r_t=K_h*\nu_t-\muobs_t\)

\[ \intd\bZ_t=\bmodel(\bZ_t,\nu_t)\intd t-\lambda\nabla\big(\widetilde K_h*\nu_t-K_h*\muobs_t\big)(\bZ_t)\intd t+\Sigma\intd\bW_t . \]
Structure preserving. If \(\bmodel=-\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\nu}\) and \(\Sigma=\sqrt{2\beta}\,I\), the assimilated flow is itself the Wasserstein gradient flow of \(\mathcal F_t(\nu)=F(\nu)+\lambda J_{\mathrm{nud},h}(\nu,\muobs_t)+\beta\,\mathrm{Ent}(\nu)\): nudging adds an observation-driven, time-dependent potential.
Smoothing by K_h restores enough regularity for the velocity to be defined when nu is only a measure, and matches the observation scale.

Part II — Multiscale Nudging

Guarantees: well posed, mean-field consistent, error contracting

PropositionsWell-posedness; propagation of chaos
\(\bmodel\) Lipschitz in \((\bx,W_2)\) and \(\nabla\widetilde K_h\) Lipschitz \(\Rightarrow\) unique strong solution for any \(T\) and \(\Law(\bZ_0)\in\mathcal P_2\). \(\|\nabla\widetilde K_h\|_\infty<\infty\) \(\Rightarrow\) the nudged \(N\)-particle system is \(f\)-chaotic: \(W_2\big(f^{1,N}_{[0,T]},f_{[0,T]}\big)\to0\). (Synchronous coupling + Grönwall; for the Gaussian kernel \(\widetilde K_h\in C^\infty\) and \(\|\nabla v-\nabla(\widetilde K_h*v)\|_{L^2}\le Ch\|D^2v\|_{L^2}\).)
Theorem\(L^2\) decay to a bias floor — \(\Omega=\Tor\), \(A=\tfrac12\Sigma\Sigma^\top\succeq\kappa I\), exact smoothed observations \(\muobs_t=K_h*\mu_t\), \(\int(\nu_0-\mu_0)=0\)
Let \(e_t=\nu_t-\mu_t\), \(V(t)=\tfrac12\|e_t\|^2_{L^2}\). Assume (C1) \(\|\bmodel(\cdot,\mu)-\bmodel(\cdot,\nu)\|_{L^2}\le L_\mu\|\mu-\nu\|_{L^2}\); (C2) \(\mu_t,\nu_t\le\bar\rho\), \(\nu_t\ge\underline\nu>0\); (C3) \(\|\nabla e_t-\nabla(\widetilde K_h*e_t)\|_{L^2}\le\delta(h)\|\nabla e_t\|_{L^2}\), \(\delta(h)\to0\). With \(C_{\rm adv}=B_{\rm model}+\bar\rho L_\mu\), model bias \(\Delta=\sup_t\|\bmodel(\cdot,\mu_t)-\btrue(\cdot,\mu_t)\|_{L^\infty}\) and Poincaré constant \(C_P\): if \[ \delta(h)\le\frac{\underline\nu}{2\bar\rho} \quad\text{and}\quad \lambda>\frac{1}{\underline\nu}\Big(\frac{2C_{\rm adv}^2C_P}{\kappa}-\kappa\Big), \qquad\text{then}\qquad V(t)\le e^{-\alpha t}V(0)+\frac{\bar\rho^{\,2}}{\alpha\kappa}\Delta^2, \qquad \alpha=\frac{\kappa+\lambda\underline\nu}{C_P}-\frac{2C_{\rm adv}^2}{\kappa}>0 . \]
Reading. Nudging contributes a dissipation \(-\lambda\underline\nu\|\nabla e\|^2\) that beats the advective growth \(C_{\rm adv}\|\nabla e\|\|e\|\) (Poincaré); (C3) says the kernel does not hide the error at scale \(h\); the long-time error is a bias floor \(\propto\Delta^2\) — zero for an exact model. Noisy or finite-sample observations add residual terms.
Larger λ beats advective instability; smaller h shrinks δ(h). Positivity of ν_t is guaranteed on finite intervals by a separate proposition.

Part III — moment nudging for Vlasov–Poisson

Plasmas: only hydrodynamic moments are observed

Vlasov–Poisson with collisions, \(\partial_t f+\bv\cdot\nabla_\bx f+\bE\cdot\nabla_\bv f=Q[f]\), \(-\Delta\phi=\rho-1\) on \(\Tor\times\mathbb R^d\). PIC: \(f^{N_p}=\sum_i w_i\,\delta_{(\bX^i,\bV^i)}\) — the particles are quadrature points of one law, not candidate states.

Observed: smoothed conserved moments (equivalently \(\rho,\bu,T\)), linear in \(f\)

\[ \big(\rho_h,\bj_h,\mathcal K_h\big)[f](\bx)=\int K_h(\bx-\by)\,\big(1,\bv,\tfrac12|\bv|^2\big)\,f(\by,\bv)\,\intd\by\,\intd\bv \] residuals \(r_0=\rho_h[f]-\rho^\obs,\ \mathbf r_1=\bj_h[f]-\bj^\obs,\ r_2=\mathcal K_h[f]-\mathcal K^\obs\), with \(\bj^\obs=\rho^\obs\bu^\obs\), \(\mathcal K^\obs=\tfrac12\rho^\obs|\bu^\obs|^2+\tfrac d2\rho^\obs T^\obs\).
Moment-compatible set \(\mathcal S_h=\{f: r_0=\mathbf r_1=r_2=0\}\ni f^\dagger\): the data fix a family, not a member. Needed: a lift from the moment residuals to a particle drift that sees the particles only through \(\mu^{N_p}\) and vanishes on all of \(\mathcal S_h\).
A two-stream state can have exactly the moments of a Maxwellian. The observations determine only the family. Nudging the moment equations directly would need a closure — the hierarchy is not closed at the observed level.
Part III — moment nudging for Vlasov–Poisson

What moment feedback cannot do: the two-Maxwellian obstruction

PropositionTwo-Maxwellian obstruction
Consider any residual-driven assimilation \(\partial_t f+\bv\cdot\nabla_\bx f+\bE[f]\cdot\nabla_\bv f=Q[f]+\mathcal N\big(f,\mathcal O[f]-\mathcal O[f^\dagger]\big)\) on the neutral torus. Assume (A1) \(Q[\mathcal M_{1,\bu,T}]=0\) for every homogeneous Maxwellian; (A2) \(\mathcal N(f,0)=0\); (A3) there are \((\bu_1,T_1)\neq(\bu_2,T_2)\) with \(\mathcal O[\mathcal M_{1,\bu_1,T_1}]=\mathcal O[\mathcal M_{1,\bu_2,T_2}]\); (A4) uniqueness. If \(f^\dagger(0)=\mathcal M_{1,\bu_1,T_1}\) and \(f(0)=\mathcal M_{1,\bu_2,T_2}\), then both stay put for all time and \(d\big(f(t),f^\dagger(t)\big)\) is constant for every metric \(d\).
CorollaryNecessity of moment observability
Synchronization from arbitrary Maxwellian initializations requires \((\bu,T)\mapsto\mathcal O[\mathcal M_{1,\bu,T}]\) to be injective: an observation map that does not distinguish bulk velocity and temperature (e.g. density alone) cannot synchronize.
Why. Linearized around a Maxwellian, moment-conserving collisions are coercive on the complement of \(\mathrm{span}\{1,\bv,|\bv|^2\}\): they damp the non-hydrodynamic shape error but leave the hydrodynamic variables untouched. What collisions do not damp, the data must observe — so the feedback is built from all three residuals \((r_0,\mathbf r_1,r_2)\).
This is a design constraint, proved before any method: any feedback that only sees residuals inherits this obstruction.
Part III — moment nudging for Vlasov–Poisson

Method A: quadratic mismatch + velocity-weighted Wasserstein metric

\[ J_M[f]=\sum_{k=0}^{2}\tfrac{\gamma_{k+1}}{2}\|r_k\|_{L^2}^2,\qquad \Phi_M[f]=\frac{\delta J_M}{\delta f}=\gamma_1K_h*r_0+\gamma_2\,\bv\cdot K_h*\mathbf r_1+\tfrac{\gamma_3}{2}|\bv|^2K_h*r_2 . \]
Plain \(W_2\) descent \(-\nabla_{\bx,\bv}\Phi_M\): the position drift contains \(\tfrac{\gamma_3}{2}|\bv|^2\nabla_\bx(K_h*r_2)\) — quadratic in \(\bv\), not globally Lipschitz, outside standard well-posedness theory.

Fix the geometry, not the functional: weight spatial transport by the peculiar speed relative to the observed flow (Benamou–Brenier)

\[ G_t(\bx,\bv)=\mathrm{diag}\Big(\big(1+\tfrac{|\bv-\bu^\obs|^2}{V_*^2}\big)I_d,\;I_d\Big), \qquad \bb_A:=-G_t^{-1}\nabla_{\bx,\bv}\Phi_M=\Big(-\frac{\nabla_\bx\Phi_M}{1+|\bv-\bu^\obs|^2/V_*^2},\;-\nabla_\bv\Phi_M\Big), \] then \(\dot{\bX}^i=\bV^i+\bb_{A,\bx}\), \(\dot{\bV}^i=\bE(\bX^i)+\bb_{A,\bv}\) (+ collision map). The weight absorbs the \(|\bv|^2\) growth; the velocity drift is affine in \(\bv\).
Two structural facts. \(\nabla_{\bx,\bv}\Phi_M[f]=0\) \(f\)-a.e. \(\iff f\in\mathcal S_h\) (the feedback vanishes exactly on the compatible set); with frozen observations \(\frac{\intd}{\intd t}J_M[f_t]=-\|\mathrm{grad}_{G}J_M\|^2_{g_{f_t}}\le0\) (Lyapunov).
The peculiar speed is Galilean invariant and equals the intrinsic one on the compatible set. V_* is a thermal speed scale.

Limits of moment feedback: the two-stream instability

Truth: \(f^\dagger_0\propto v^2e^{-v^2/2}(1+\alpha\cos k_0x)\), moments \((\rho,u,T)=(1,0,3)\); weak BGK collisions (\(\nu=0.05\)), no driver — the instability saturates into a phase-space vortex. Two priors with the same wrong moments \((u,T)=(0.3,2)\): a Maxwellian (wrong shape) and the affinely shifted two-stream state (right shape).

Maxwellian prior: A/B synchronize \(u,T\) (\(R_u=0.21\), \(R_T=0.13\)), but the final marginal is a Maxwellian with the observed moments — not bimodal.
Two-stream prior: the moments are corrected while the structure is kept; the nudged runs develop the instability and track the true vortex (\(R_f=0.48\) for A and B).
Consistent with the obstruction: with weak collisions and a truth held far from equilibrium by its own field, the shape is neither observed nor damped. Feedback fixes what is observed; the model must supply the rest.
