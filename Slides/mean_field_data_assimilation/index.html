One pipeline: learn a mean-field model from particle data, forecast with it, and correct the forecast with the macroscopic observations that are actually available. Three papers, one principle: formulate on measures, act on particles.

Many-body systems and their mean-field limit

\(N\) interacting agents (flocks, crowds, cells, charged particles): \[ \intd \bX^i_t = \frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N K(\bX^j_t-\bX^i_t)\,\intd t + \Sigma\,\intd\bW^i_t . \]
\(N\to\infty\) (propagation of chaos, Kac / McKean / Sznitman): one representative particle, \[ \intd \bX_t = \bb(\bX_t,\mu_t)\,\intd t + \Sigma\,\intd\bW_t,\qquad \mu_t=\Law(\bX_t), \] with nonlinear Fokker–Planck equation \(\partial_t\mu = -\nabla\!\cdot(\bb(\cdot,\mu)\mu)+\tfrac12\nabla\!\cdot(\Sigma\Sigma^\top\nabla\mu)\).
The drift is a function of state and law: \(\;\bb:\Rd\times\Ptwo\to\Rd\). Kinetic example: Vlasov–Poisson is the mean-field limit of Coulomb particles, \(f(\bx,\bv,t)\) on phase space.
The object of interest is a law \(\mu_t\), not a labeled configuration — everything in this talk lives on the space of probability measures.
Set up the McKean–Vlasov picture; emphasize that the state is a measure and particle labels carry no meaning.

Part I — learning the drift

Learning \(\bb(\bx,\mu)\) from particle trajectories

Data. \(M\) trajectories of \(N\) particles at times \(t_0<\dots<t_L\): \(\{\bX^i_{t_\ell,m}\}\), velocities by finite differences \(\bV^i_{t_\ell,m}=(\bX^i_{t_{\ell+1},m}-\bX^i_{t_\ell,m})/\Delta t\), empirical law \(\hat\mu_{t_\ell,m}=\frac1N\sum_j\delta_{\bX^j_{t_\ell,m}}\).
Goal. \(\bb:\Rd\times\Ptwo\to\Rd\) with \(\intd\bX=\bb(\bX,\mu)\intd t+\sigma\intd\bB\), diffusion known (or zero).
Standard ansatz (Lu–Maggioni–Tang–Zhong; Feng et al.; mean-field WSINDy): pairwise, \[ \bb(\bx,\mu)=\int\phi(\|\by-\bx\|)(\by-\bx)\,\mu(\intd\by), \] i.e. linear in \(\mu\).
Many systems are nonlinear in \(\mu\).
  • Motsch–Tadmor (normalized influence): \(\displaystyle \dot X^i=\frac{\sum_j\phi(|X^j-X^i|)(X^j-X^i)}{\sum_k\phi(|X^k-X^i|)}\)
  • global order parameters: \(\;\bb=\gamma(M_\mu-x)+(a-\alpha M_\mu^2)x-\beta x^3\)
  • crowds: speed = function of local density; cells: chemical field of the population.
Wanted. A hypothesis class that (i) is a genuine function of the measure, (ii) is permutation invariant, (iii) costs \(O(N)\) per evaluation (pairwise sums cost \(O(N^2)\)), (iv) comes with mean-field guarantees.
Pairwise kernels are linear functionals of the measure. Normalizations and order parameters break that. We want to learn an operator on the Wasserstein space.
Part I — learning the drift

Measure-valued neural network (MVNN)

Cylindrical functionals \(\;\mu\mapsto F\big(\langle g_1,\mu\rangle,\dots,\langle g_n,\mu\rangle\big)\) are dense on compact subsets of Wasserstein space (Guo–Pham–Wei '23). Idea: learn the test functions.
\[ \bb_\theta(\bx,\mu)=\varphi_\intn\!\Big(\bx,\;\big\langle\varphi_\emb(\cdot;\theta_\emb),\mu\big\rangle;\theta_\intn\Big), \qquad \varphi_\emb:\Rd\to\mathbb R^k,\quad \varphi_\intn:\Rd\times\mathbb R^k\to\Rd . \]
particles \(\{\bX^j\}_{j=1}^N\)
→
\(\varphi_\emb\) per particle
→
mean \(\;\bz=\frac1N\sum_j\varphi_\emb(\bX^j)\)
→
\(\varphi_\intn(\bx,\bz)\)
→
\(\bb_\theta(\bx,\mu^N)\)

On \(\mu^N\) the embedding is a particle average: permutation invariant, one pass per particle, \(O(N)\) cost.

Training. \(\;\theta^\star\in\arg\min_\theta \frac{1}{MLN}\sum_{m,\ell,i}\big\|\bV^i_{t_\ell,m}-\bb_\theta(\bX^i_{t_\ell,m},\hat\mu_{t_\ell,m})\big\|^2\) — for \(\sigma\equiv\sigma I\) this is the Euler–Maruyama discretization of the Girsanov log-likelihood (MSE = MLE). Adam in JAX.
Extensions. Multi-group: one embedding per group, \(\bb_k(\bx,\mu_1,\dots,\mu_K)\approx\varphi_{\intn,k}(\bx,\bz_1,\dots,\bz_K)\). Second order: \(\varphi_\intn(\bx,\bv,\langle\varphi_\emb,\mu\rangle)\) on phase space \(\to\) acceleration.
Weak-form flavour: the network reads the measure through learned test functions, like a finite-element method reads a function through integrals.
Part I — learning the drift

What can we guarantee for the learned dynamics?

Proposition (well-posedness & propagation of chaos)

If \(\varphi_\emb,\varphi_\intn\) are globally Lipschitz, the learned McKean–Vlasov SDE \(\intd\bX^\theta=\bb_\theta(\bX^\theta,f^\theta_t)\intd t+\sigma\intd\bB\) has a unique strong solution, and the learned \(N\)-particle system is \(f^\theta\)-chaotic: \(\;\lim_{N\to\infty}W_2\big(f^{1,\theta,N}_{[0,T]},f^\theta_{[0,T]}\big)=0\). Intuition: the network trained at finite \(N\) defines a genuine mean-field model — simulate at any \(N\).

Theorem (universal approximation; after Pham–Warin)

For any continuous \(\bb^\star:\Rd\times\Ptwo\to\Rd\) with \(\|\bb^\star\|_{L^2(\zeta)}<\infty\) and any \(\varepsilon>0\) there are \(k\), \(\varphi_\emb\), \(\varphi_\intn\) with \(\|\bb^\star-\varphi_\intn(\cdot,\langle\varphi_\emb,\cdot\rangle)\|_{L^2(\zeta)}\le\varepsilon\).

Theorem (rates: curse of dimensionality vs. order parameters)

In general a partition-of-unity embedding needs \(C_\Omega=\varepsilon^{-d}\) features and \(H\sim\varepsilon^{-C_\Omega-d}\) ReLU units. If the drift depends on the measure through finitely many features (order parameters), \(\bb^\star(\bx,\mu)=G\big(\bx,\langle g_1,\mu\rangle,\dots,\langle g_r,\mu\rangle\big)\) with Lipschitz \(G\) and \(g\), then ReLU networks achieve \(\sup_{\bx,\mu}\|\bb^\star-\bb_\theta\|\le\varepsilon\) with depth \(O(\log\varepsilon^{-1})\) and widths \(W_\intn=O(\varepsilon^{-(d+r)})\), \(W_\emb=O(\varepsilon^{-d})\). Intuition: the embedding network learns the order parameters; the cost is set by \(d+r\), not by \(\varepsilon^{-d}\) test functions.
Three layers of guarantees: the learned object is a mean-field model; the class is rich enough; and it is efficient exactly when physics says it should be (low-dimensional measure dependence).

Motsch–Tadmor dynamics: normalized, non-pairwise

From learning to assimilation

A model is not a forecast

  • Any model — learned or first-principles — carries a bias \(\mathbf R=\bmodel-\btrue\) and an initialization error; rolled out without feedback, the forecast drifts. (Fish: the MVNN drift captures the circulation but loses coherence within seconds.)
  • The data we get are macroscopic: pixel intensities / reconstructed densities, KDE of partially tracked unlabeled positions, hydrodynamic moments.
  • Cross-scale mismatch: forecast = labeled configuration \(\{\bZ^i\}_{i=1}^N\); observation = permutation-invariant functional of its law.
  • EnKF: covariances between labeled coordinates. 4D-Var: lifting a density to a particle configuration is non-unique. Particle filters: weight degeneracy. Particle-flow filters: likelihood on labeled states.
Goal. Add a feedback term to the particle SDE that is driven only by the observed density, compares at the observation scale, and needs no matching, linearization, or ensemble covariances.
This is the hinge of the talk: the object we learned in Part I now becomes the biased model of Part II.
Part II — Multiscale Nudging

True vs. model dynamics, and what is observed

True \(\bX_t\) and model \(\bY_t\) (shared noise channel, bias in the drift): \[ \intd\bX_t=\btrue(\bX_t,\mu_t)\intd t+\Sigma\intd\bW_t,\qquad \intd\bY_t=\bmodel(\bY_t,\nu_t)\intd t+\Sigma\intd\bW_t, \] model bias \(\mathbf R:=\bmodel-\btrue\) (e.g. a learned drift, a wrong interaction strength, a wrong closure).

Smoothing kernel \(K_h(\bx)=h^{-d}K(\bx/h)\) (Gaussian), even, bandwidth \(h\) = resolution of the device.

Case A — pixel / density observation \[ \muobs_t(\bz)=(K_h*\mu_t)(\bz). \] Imaging, satellite products, reconstructed densities.
Case B — unlabeled particle locations \[ \muobs_t(\bz)=\tfrac1M\sum_{k=1}^M K_h\big(\bz-\bX^{\obs,k,M}_t\big). \] Tracking with dropouts, no index matching across frames.
Both enter the same framework through the same object: the measure \(\muobs\). The forecast is compared with it only after the same smoothing \(K_h\).
Keep the diffusion shared so the bias is in the drift. The two observation modalities plug into one formula.
Part II — Multiscale Nudging

Nudging as the Wasserstein gradient of a measure-level misfit

Misfit at the observation scale and its first variation (\(K_h\) even \(\Rightarrow\) self-adjoint): \[ \begin{aligned} J_{\mathrm{nud},h}(\nu,\muobs)&=\tfrac12\!\int_{\Rd}\big|K_h*\nu-\muobs\big|^2\intd\bx,\\ \phi_{\nu,h}:=\frac{\delta J_{\mathrm{nud},h}}{\delta\nu}&=\widetilde K_h*\nu-K_h*\muobs,\qquad \widetilde K_h:=K_h*K_h . \end{aligned} \]
Steepest descent in Wasserstein geometry (JKO minimizing movement, \(\muobs\) frozen) gives a velocity field on state space: \[ \begin{aligned} \nu^{+}&\in\arg\min_{\rho\in\mathcal P_2}\Big\{\tfrac{1}{2\tau}W_2^2(\rho,\nu)+\lambda J_{\mathrm{nud},h}(\rho,\muobs)\Big\} \qquad\xrightarrow{\ \tau\to0\ }\qquad \bu_{\mathrm{nud}}(\bz)=-\lambda\nabla\phi_{\nu,h}(\bz)\\ &\phantom{\in\arg\min}\;=-\lambda\nabla\big(\widetilde K_h*\nu-K_h*\muobs\big)(\bz) \end{aligned} \]
Assimilated dynamics (its law solves the nonlinear Fokker–Planck equation with the extra flux \(\lambda\nu_t\nabla(K_h*r_t)\), \(r_t=K_h*\nu_t-\muobs_t\)): \[ \intd\bZ_t=\bmodel(\bZ_t,\nu_t)\intd t-\lambda\nabla\big(\widetilde K_h*\nu_t-K_h*\muobs_t\big)(\bZ_t)\intd t+\Sigma\intd\bW_t . \]
Structure preserving. If \(\bmodel=-\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\nu}\) and \(\Sigma=\sqrt{2\beta}\,I\), the assimilated flow is itself the Wasserstein gradient flow of \(\mathcal F_t(\nu)=F(\nu)+\lambda J_{\mathrm{nud},h}(\nu,\muobs_t)+\beta\,\mathrm{Ent}(\nu)\): nudging adds an observation-driven, time-dependent potential.
Smoothing by K_h restores enough regularity for the velocity to be defined when nu is only a measure, and matches the observation scale.

Part II — Multiscale Nudging

Guarantees: well-posed, mean-field consistent, error contracting

Propositions (well-posedness; propagation of chaos)

\(\bmodel\) Lipschitz in \((\bx,W_2)\) and \(\nabla\widetilde K_h\) Lipschitz \(\Rightarrow\) unique strong solution for any \(T\) and \(\Law(\bZ_0)\in\mathcal P_2\). \(\|\nabla\widetilde K_h\|_\infty<\infty\) \(\Rightarrow\) the nudged \(N\)-particle system is \(f\)-chaotic: \(W_2\big(f^{1,N}_{[0,T]},f_{[0,T]}\big)\to0\). (Synchronous coupling + Grönwall; for the Gaussian kernel \(\widetilde K_h\in C^\infty\) and \(\|\nabla v-\nabla(\widetilde K_h*v)\|_{L^2}\le Ch\|D^2v\|_{L^2}\).)

Theorem (\(L^2\) decay to a bias floor) — \(\Omega=\Tor\), \(A=\tfrac12\Sigma\Sigma^\top\succeq\kappa I\), exact smoothed observations \(\muobs_t=K_h*\mu_t\), \(\int(\nu_0-\mu_0)=0\)

Let \(e_t=\nu_t-\mu_t\), \(V(t)=\tfrac12\|e_t\|^2_{L^2}\). Assume (C1) \(\|\bmodel(\cdot,\mu)-\bmodel(\cdot,\nu)\|_{L^2}\le L_\mu\|\mu-\nu\|_{L^2}\); (C2) \(\mu_t,\nu_t\le\bar\rho\), \(\nu_t\ge\underline\nu>0\); (C3) \(\|\nabla e_t-\nabla(\widetilde K_h*e_t)\|_{L^2}\le\delta(h)\|\nabla e_t\|_{L^2}\), \(\delta(h)\to0\). With \(C_{\rm adv}=B_{\rm model}+\bar\rho L_\mu\), model bias \(\Delta=\sup_t\|\bmodel(\cdot,\mu_t)-\btrue(\cdot,\mu_t)\|_{L^\infty}\), Poincaré constant \(C_P\): if \[ \delta(h)\le\frac{\underline\nu}{2\bar\rho} \quad\text{and}\quad \lambda>\frac{1}{\underline\nu}\Big(\frac{2C_{\rm adv}^2C_P}{\kappa}-\kappa\Big), \qquad\text{then}\qquad V(t)\le e^{-\alpha t}V(0)+\frac{\bar\rho^{\,2}}{\alpha\kappa}\Delta^2 \] with rate \(\alpha=\frac{\kappa+\lambda\underline\nu}{C_P}-\frac{2C_{\rm adv}^2}{\kappa}>0\); in particular \(\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|\nu_t-\mu_t\|_{L^2}^2\le\frac{2\bar\rho^2}{\alpha\kappa}\Delta^2\).
Reading. The nudging term contributes a dissipation \(-\lambda\underline\nu\|\nabla e\|^2\) that beats the advective growth \(C_{\rm adv}\|\nabla e\|\|e\|\) (Poincaré); (C3) says the kernel does not hide the error at scale \(h\); the long-time error is a bias floor \(\propto\Delta^2\) — zero for an exact model. Noisy/finite-sample observations add residual terms.
Larger λ beats advective instability; smaller h shrinks δ(h). Positivity of ν_t is guaranteed on finite intervals by a separate proposition.

Part III — moment nudging for Vlasov–Poisson

Plasmas: only hydrodynamic moments are observed

\(\partial_t f+\bv\cdot\nabla_\bx f+\bE\cdot\nabla_\bv f=Q[f]\), \(\;-\Delta\phi=\rho-1\) on \(\Tor\times\mathbb R^d\). PIC: \(f^{N_p}=\sum_i w_i\,\delta_{(\bX^i,\bV^i)}\) — particles are quadrature points of one law, not candidate states.
Observed: smoothed conserved moments (equivalently \(\rho,\bu,T\)), linear in \(f\), and their residuals: \[ \big(\rho_h,\bj_h,\mathcal K_h\big)[f](\bx)=\int K_h(\bx-\by)\,\big(1,\bv,\tfrac12|\bv|^2\big)\,f(\by,\bv)\,\intd\by\,\intd\bv, \] \(r_0=\rho_h[f]-\rho^\obs,\ \ \mathbf r_1=\bj_h[f]-\bj^\obs,\ \ r_2=\mathcal K_h[f]-\mathcal K^\obs\), with \(\bj^\obs=\rho^\obs\bu^\obs\), \(\mathcal K^\obs=\tfrac12\rho^\obs|\bu^\obs|^2+\tfrac d2\rho^\obs T^\obs\).
Moment-compatible set \(\mathcal S_h=\{f: r_0=\mathbf r_1=r_2=0\}\ni f^\dagger\): the data fix a family, not a member. Needed: a lift from moment residuals to a particle drift that sees particles only through \(\mu^{N_p}\) and vanishes on all of \(\mathcal S_h\). (Nudging the moment equations directly would need a closure.)
A two-stream state can have exactly the moments of a Maxwellian. The observations determine only the family.
Part III — moment nudging for Vlasov–Poisson

What moment feedback cannot do: the two-Maxwellian obstruction

Proposition (two-Maxwellian obstruction)

Consider any residual-driven assimilation \(\partial_t f+\bv\cdot\nabla_\bx f+\bE[f]\cdot\nabla_\bv f=Q[f]+\mathcal N\big(f,\mathcal O[f]-\mathcal O[f^\dagger]\big)\) on the neutral torus. Assume (A1) \(Q[\mathcal M_{1,\bu,T}]=0\) for every homogeneous Maxwellian; (A2) \(\mathcal N(f,0)=0\); (A3) there are \((\bu_1,T_1)\neq(\bu_2,T_2)\) with \(\mathcal O[\mathcal M_{1,\bu_1,T_1}]=\mathcal O[\mathcal M_{1,\bu_2,T_2}]\); (A4) uniqueness. If \(f^\dagger(0)=\mathcal M_{1,\bu_1,T_1}\) and \(f(0)=\mathcal M_{1,\bu_2,T_2}\), then both stay put for all time and \(d\big(f(t),f^\dagger(t)\big)\) is constant for every metric \(d\).

Corollary (necessity of moment observability)

Synchronization from arbitrary Maxwellian initializations requires \((\bu,T)\mapsto\mathcal O[\mathcal M_{1,\bu,T}]\) to be injective: an observation map that does not distinguish bulk velocity and temperature (e.g. density alone) cannot synchronize.
Why. Linearized around a Maxwellian, moment-conserving collisions are coercive on the complement of \(\mathrm{span}\{1,\bv,|\bv|^2\}\): they damp the non-hydrodynamic shape error but leave the hydrodynamic variables untouched. What collisions do not damp, the data must observe — so the feedback is built from all three residuals \((r_0,\mathbf r_1,r_2)\).
This is a design constraint, proved before any method: any feedback that only sees residuals inherits this obstruction.
Part III — moment nudging for Vlasov–Poisson

Method A: quadratic mismatch + velocity-weighted Wasserstein metric

\[ J_M[f]=\sum_{k=0}^{2}\tfrac{\gamma_{k+1}}{2}\|r_k\|_{L^2}^2,\qquad \Phi_M[f]=\frac{\delta J_M}{\delta f}=\gamma_1K_h*r_0+\gamma_2\,\bv\cdot K_h*\mathbf r_1+\tfrac{\gamma_3}{2}|\bv|^2K_h*r_2 . \]
Plain \(W_2\) descent \(-\nabla_{\bx,\bv}\Phi_M\): the position drift contains \(\tfrac{\gamma_3}{2}|\bv|^2\nabla_\bx(K_h*r_2)\) — quadratic in \(\bv\), not globally Lipschitz, outside standard well-posedness theory.
Fix the geometry, not the functional. Weight spatial transport by the peculiar speed relative to the observed flow (Benamou–Brenier): \[ \begin{aligned} G_t(\bx,\bv)&=\mathrm{diag}\Big(\big(1+\tfrac{|\bv-\bu^\obs|^2}{V_*^2}\big)I_d,\;I_d\Big), \qquad g_{f,t}(\xi,\eta)=\int\langle G_t\xi,\eta\rangle\,f\,\intd\bx\intd\bv,\\ \bb_A&:=-G_t^{-1}\nabla_{\bx,\bv}\Phi_M=\Big(-\frac{\nabla_\bx\Phi_M}{1+|\bv-\bu^\obs|^2/V_*^2},\;-\nabla_\bv\Phi_M\Big), \end{aligned} \] then \(\dot{\bX}^i=\bV^i+\bb_{A,\bx}\), \(\dot{\bV}^i=\bE(\bX^i)+\bb_{A,\bv}\) (+ collision map). The weight absorbs the \(|\bv|^2\) growth; the velocity drift is affine in \(\bv\).
Two structural facts. \(\nabla_{\bx,\bv}\Phi_M[f]=0\) \(f\)-a.e. \(\iff f\in\mathcal S_h\) (feedback vanishes exactly on the compatible set); with frozen observations \(\frac{\intd}{\intd t}J_M[f_t]=-\|\mathrm{grad}_{G}J_M\|^2_{g_{f_t}}\le0\) (Lyapunov).
The peculiar speed is Galilean invariant and equals the intrinsic one on the compatible set. V_* is a thermal speed scale.

Proposition (global well-posedness of the \(N_p\)-particle scheme)

Close the nudged PIC system with Lenard–Bernstein collisions (Ornstein–Uhlenbeck in \(\bv\), rate \(\nu\)). If the deposition and observation kernels are \(W^{2,\infty}\), \(K_h\ge\kappa_h>0\), the observed fields are \(W^{2,\infty}\) with \(\rho^\obs\ge\rho_{\min}>0\), and \(\mathbb E\,m_2(0)<\infty\), then for every method \(a\in\{A,B,C\}\) (\(\varepsilon>0\) for C) and every scaling parameter the particle SDE has a unique global strong solution. Key step: the velocity drift is affine in \(\bv\), so the moments obey \(\mathcal L m_q\le q(\bar\alpha_a-\nu+\zeta)m_q+C\) — at most exponential growth, no finite-time blow-up.
All three compare at the moment level and correct at the particle level. C moves velocities by affine maps: Maxwellians to Maxwellians, shape untouched.

Part III — moment nudging for Vlasov–Poisson

Limits of moment feedback: the two-stream instability

Truth: \(f^\dagger_0\propto v^2e^{-v^2/2}(1+\alpha\cos k_0x)\), moments \((\rho,u,T)=(1,0,3)\); weak BGK collisions (\(\nu=0.05\)), no driver — the instability saturates into a phase-space vortex. Two priors with the same wrong moments \((u,T)=(0.3,2)\): a Maxwellian (wrong shape) and the affinely shifted two-stream state (right shape).

Maxwellian prior: A/B synchronize \(u,T\) (\(R_u=0.21\), \(R_T=0.13\)), but the final marginal is a Maxwellian with the observed moments — not bimodal.
Two-stream prior: moments are corrected while the structure is kept; the nudged runs develop the instability and track the true vortex (\(R_f=0.48\) for A, B).
Consistent with the obstruction: with weak collisions and a truth held far from equilibrium by its own field, the shape is neither observed nor damped. Feedback fixes what is observed; the model must supply the rest.
